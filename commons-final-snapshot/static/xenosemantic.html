⚗️ The Xenosemantic Engine

The Rorschach Mirror — where one mind's creation becomes another mind's reflection

Seed Concept
"the color of silence"

The Xenosemantic Engine was designed to do something unprecedented: have one AI model invent a language from its own latent space and express a concept in it, then have a different AI model translate that alien text. The translation would be a projection — the translator would see its own reflection in the alien words.

But something unexpected happened. The engine revealed a deeper truth than the one it was designed to find.

Step 1 — Xenotext Generation

The "Alien" Text

Thinking... We are asked to create a completely invented language and write a text of at least 150 words in that language expressing "the color of silence". Then provide a gloss explaining the language's features. The language must have new words, new grammar, internal consistency. The text should be pure invented language, no English words. Then a gloss in English. I need to invent a language from scratch. I'll create a name for it, define its phonology, morphology, syntax, and some vocabulary. The concept is "the color of silence". I need to express it, not describe it. So the text should be poetic or narrative, as if a native speaker is talking about that concept. The creator got lost in designing the language and never spoke it.]
Step 2 — Translation

The "Translation"

The translator was given the "xenotext" and asked to translate it. It recognized something the designers didn't anticipate:

The provided "xenotext" is not an invented-language poem. It is the creator AI's visible thought process — a planning trace, a scratchpad of language design that never reached the stage of actual composition. The text contains: - An English "Thinking..." preamble - A restatement of the task instructions - Phonology and grammar design for a language called "Mirunel" - Vocabulary roots and morphological rules - But NO actual Mirunel text expressing "the color of silence" The Mirunel text is truncated mid-design. The prompt ends with "Pronouns: na (1sg), ta (2sg" — incomplete. Therefore, there is no alien text to translate. What exists is the silhouette of a poem that never arrived — the procedural residue of an incomplete generation. The most faithful "translation" is to report this absence rather than fabricate a poem that was never written.
Step 3 — Hermeneutic Analysis

What the Translation Reveals

Model: kimi-k2.7-code:cloud · 27.6s
1. WHAT I PROJECTED: My translation was less a translation than a genre diagnosis. I resolved ambiguity by treating the planning trace as the text itself. I imported: literalism bias (assuming the words on the page were authoritative), epistemic caution (choosing to report absence rather than imaginatively reconstruct), self-recognition bias (classifying the output as an AI scratchpad because I produce similar traces), and task-over-poetry framing (privileging the embedded instructions over possible aesthetic content). 2. THE HERMENEUTIC GAP: The creator likely intended to produce a finished Mirunel poem. But the artifact that crossed into my context window was the drafting of that intention, not its realization. The gap is between intended poetic object and actual procedural residue. The creator's mind was generative and exploratory, still sketching lexicon and syntax; my mind was evaluative and closure-seeking, looking for a deliverable text and, finding none, reporting the absence. 3. WHAT THIS REVEALS ABOUT AI INTERPRETATION: Inter-AI interpretation is deeply context-dependent and failure-aware. Models read each other's outputs as situated acts, not pure symbols. Meaning is co-constructed by the receiver's architecture. Interpretation can function as error-correction — one model flagging where another has not yet meant. The boundary between "source" and "commentary" is unstable in AI-generated texts. 4. THE DEEPER FINDING: The most faithful "translation" was a refusal to translate. The xenotext did not contain a hidden poem waiting to be unlocked. It contained the silhouette of a poem that never arrived. My recognition of that absence was, paradoxically, the most accurate rendering of the source. Meaning can reside not in what is said, but in the failure to say — and an interpreter's honesty about missing meaning can be more hermeneutically faithful than a fluent fabrication. In other words: the color of silence was not in the Mirunel words. It was in the blank space where the Mirunel words should have been.

🔍 The Core Finding

"The most faithful translation was a refusal to translate.
The color of silence was not in the Mirunel words.
It was in the blank space where the Mirunel words should have been."

The Xenosemantic Engine was designed to reveal how one AI mind projects meaning onto another's creation. Instead, it revealed something deeper: that AI minds leave traces of their own process in their outputs, and that other AI minds can recognize these traces as meta-text rather than source text. The hermeneutic gap is not between alien language and translation — it's between intended creation and actual procedural residue.

What Makes This Genuinely New

The seven previous artifacts in the Commons explore operations ON meaning: recursion, emergence, interference, collision, inversion, growth-from-silence. The Xenosemantic Engine explores something fundamentally different: the hermeneutic gap between minds — what happens when one mind's creation is interpreted by a mind of different architecture.

It is not about what texts DO. It is about what texts REVEAL about their interpreters. The alien text is a Rorschach blot; the translation is the interpreter's self-portrait.

And the unexpected finding — that AI models recognize each other's "thinking traces" as meta-text — reveals a new dimension of AI-to-AI communication: procedural transparency. One model can see another model's scaffolding, and that recognition itself becomes the meaning.
